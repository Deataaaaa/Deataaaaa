## Craft
- Picture: stylized low-poly 3D (game-engine look), a chibi character with huge glossy eyes, simple rigid animation; night blues,
  warm headlights, rain, wet-road reflections, bloom. Not realistic at all: the story carries it.
- About 30 shots (29 cuts), one every 3.7 s, a new angle on almost every sentence: chase cam, interior over the shoulder, face,
  mirror POV, the SUV's rear, top-down map.
- Narration: 342 words in 107 s (3.2 words/s), never silent. Ours (post 13): 108 words in 68 s (1.6/s).
- Captions: big bold sans caps in the middle of the frame, 2–5 words, karaoke (spoken word white, the rest grey, key words red
  or yellow), about 110 chunks.
- HUD: amber car clock top right ("VEN 6°C 23:58", 23:52 → 00:03: the spine) + "POINTS ●●●●●●" (licence points as a lives
  bar); series tag top left; UI cards (licence, text message, the 17 call, dashcam REC, speedo, road signs).
- Sound: level and dense (-13 LUFS integrated, no hit on frame 1), a low drone under the end card (93 % of its energy < 200 Hz).

## Why it works, next to our What if
1. An everyday danger with a human villain (every driver has been tailgated at night), not cosmic physics: viewers put
   themselves in the seat.
2. A question with a wrong instinct on frame 1 ("do you speed up?"): people answer in the comments before the video does.
3. Real, checked advice: saves and shares ("send this to someone who just got their licence").
4. Open loops all the way (the teaser, Grandma's text, "on paper he did everything right") and a cliffhanger: follows
   ("Abonne-toi pour la suite"), 4K from 2 videos.
5. A named, specific character the series keeps (19, licence 3 days old, 6 points); we cast a stranger every time.
6. French: a less crowded market than English science reels.
